# The Illustrated Transformer

**Who:** Jay Alammar, independent blogger at the time (later co-author of the book "Hands-On Large Language Models")
**What / when:** blog post, June 27, 2018, jalammar.github.io
**Full piece:** https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/
**Jump in here:** https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/#self-attention-at-a-high-level (section "Self-Attention at a High Level", six short paragraphs plus one figure, ~2 min)

## Why this one

Probably the most widely used visual explainer of the Transformer: the page itself says it's featured in courses at Stanford, Harvard, MIT, Princeton and CMU, and has been translated into a dozen languages. Foundational era after Friday's current DeepSeek-R1 pick, and a new person. It's a blog post again (last one was Karpathy on 11-19), but all remaining foundational candidates are papers or posts, and this one fits the education angle better than any of them. The "Self-Attention at a High Level" section is a small example of good teaching: the author admits he'd never heard of self-attention before the paper, then explains it with a single ambiguous sentence about an animal and a street.

## What's said there

> "Don’t be fooled by me throwing around the word “self-attention” like it’s a concept everyone should be familiar with. I had personally never came across the concept until reading the Attention is All You Need paper." (section "Self-Attention at a High Level", first paragraph, text verified: yes; "never came across" is in the original)

> "What does “it” in this sentence refer to? Is it referring to the street or to the animal? It’s a simple question to a human, but not as simple to an algorithm." (same section, text verified: yes)

- Example sentence: "The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired". When the model processes "it", self-attention lets it associate "it" with "animal".
- More generally: for each word, self-attention lets the model look at other positions in the input for clues that lead to a better encoding of that word.
- He connects it to what readers may know: like an RNN's hidden state carrying earlier words along, self-attention is how the Transformer will "bake the “understanding” of other relevant words into the one we’re currently processing." (verified)
- The figure shows attention from "it" in the top encoder (#5) focusing on "The Animal".

Alternative bit in the intro, for a then-vs-now angle (verified): "The biggest benefit, however, comes from how The Transformer lends itself to parallelization." In 2018 the Transformer is framed as a machine translation model that trains faster.
