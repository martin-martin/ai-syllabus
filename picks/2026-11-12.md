## Why this one

Temperature is a setting many people have seen in a chatbot or API config without knowing what it does. Sanderson shows it in about 90 seconds: one constant in the softmax, then the same GPT-3 story prompt at two temperatures side by side. It ties the math directly to behavior anyone who has used an LLM has noticed, and it's a nice example of his "show it, then name it" teaching style.

## What's said there

> "Temperature zero means that it always goes with the most predictable word, and what you get ends up being a trite derivative of Goldilocks." (24:43, transcript verified: yes)

> "Technically speaking, the API doesn't actually let you pick a temperature bigger than 2. There's no mathematical reason for this, it's just an arbitrary constraint imposed to keep their tool from being seen generating things that are too nonsensical." (25:06, transcript verified: yes)

- Softmax (22:31) turns the model's raw output numbers into a probability distribution over next tokens: exponentiate, then divide by the sum.
- Temperature is a constant T in the denominator of those exponents. Larger T flattens the distribution and gives unlikely words more weight; T = 0 puts all weight on the most likely word.
- Demo with GPT-3 and the seed "once upon a time there was A": at temperature zero a Goldilocks retread; at a higher temperature a more original start ("a young web artist from South Korea") that "quickly degenerates into nonsense."
- He notes the API caps temperature at 2 and calls that cap arbitrary rather than mathematical.

**C, longer, educator's angle:**

If you've ever wondered what the "temperature" slider in an LLM tool actually does, this section from Grant Sanderson (3Blue1Brown) is a good one to share.

He first walks through softmax, the step that turns the model's raw numbers into probabilities for the next word. Then he adds a single constant T into the exponents and shows what changes: a large T spreads the probability across less likely words, a small T lets the top choice dominate, and T = 0 always picks the most likely word.

Then he just runs it. Same GPT-3 prompt, "once upon a time there was A", two temperatures. Cold, it's a Goldilocks retread. Warmer, it starts with a young web artist from South Korea and soon falls apart.
