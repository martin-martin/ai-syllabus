**Who:** Jeff Dean, Google DeepMind / Google Research

## Why this one

The whole talk covers 15 years of ideas behind today's models. This short section is the origin story: a 1990 undergrad thesis on training neural nets on 32 processors, then 20+ years later a chance meeting with Andrew Ng that led to Google Brain. It's a nice reminder that "scale" was an idea long before it was affordable, and it's an easy story to retell when teaching.

## What's said there

> "So I saw these as an undergrad in 1990 and I got super excited and I said, 'Oh wow, that seems like a really good abstraction. It feels like it's a trainable thing and it can learn almost anything.'" (06:26, transcript verified: yes)

- Dean's senior thesis (with Vipin Kumar) tried two ways to train neural nets in parallel, what we'd now call model parallel and data parallel training. He called them "pattern partitioned" and "pipelined".
- The speedups tapered off because he kept the model size fixed instead of growing it with the number of processors. He "filed that away" and ignored it for about 25 years.
- At Google he bumped into Andrew Ng in a micro kitchen. Ng's Stanford students were training neural nets for speech and vision, and Dean suggested training really big ones on Google's computers. That became the start of Google Brain (DistBelief).
