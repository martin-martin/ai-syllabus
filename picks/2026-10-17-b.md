# Understanding LSTM Networks

**Who:** Chris Olah, Google (at the time; later Anthropic co-founder)
**What / when:** Blog post, 2015-08-27, colah's blog
**Full piece:** https://colah.github.io/posts/2015-08-Understanding-LSTMs/
**Jump in here:** https://colah.github.io/posts/2015-08-Understanding-LSTMs/#conclusion (section "Conclusion", 4 paragraphs, ~2 min; optional warm-up: "The Core Idea Behind LSTMs", #the-core-idea-behind-lstms)

## Why this one

Probably the most-shared explanation of LSTMs ever written, and a model of how to teach a scary set of equations with diagrams and one running example. The conclusion is a small time capsule: in 2015 the field's guess for the "next step" after LSTMs was attention, and two years later the transformer paper dropped recurrence altogether. Foundational era and written format, as a contrast to the previous pick (Jeff Dean, current-era video talk).

## What's said there

> "LSTMs were a big step in what we can accomplish with RNNs. It's natural to wonder: is there another big step? A common opinion among researchers is: "Yes! There is a next step and it's attention!"" (section "Conclusion", text verified: yes)

- Olah notes that essentially all the remarkable RNN results of the time were achieved with LSTMs.
- He admits that written as equations LSTMs "look pretty intimidating" and hopes the step-by-step walkthrough makes them approachable.
- He describes attention as letting every step of an RNN pick information to look at from a larger collection, e.g. picking a part of an image for each word of a caption (Xu et al., 2015).
- Earlier in the post he frames the cell state as "kind of like a conveyor belt", with gates (sigmoid layer plus pointwise multiplication) controlling what is removed or added.
