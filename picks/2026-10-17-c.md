# From Words to Worlds: Spatial Intelligence is AI's Next Frontier

**Who:** Fei-Fei Li, Stanford professor and co-founder of World Labs
**What / when:** essay, 2025-11-10, her Substack
**Full piece:** https://drfeifei.substack.com/p/from-words-to-worlds-spatial-intelligence
**Jump in here:** https://drfeifei.substack.com/p/from-words-to-worlds-spatial-intelligence#:~:text=Unfortunately%2C%20today (section "Spatial Intelligence: The scaffolding of human cognition", the four paragraphs from "Unfortunately, today's AI doesn't think like this yet." up to the Wittgenstein line, ~3 min read)

## Why this one

Fei-Fei Li built ImageNet, the dataset behind the 2012 deep learning breakthrough, and here she argues that the next gap to close isn't language but space: distances, orientation, physics. It links a foundational piece of AI history (her own) to a current debate about world models, and it's written for a general audience, with everyday examples instead of equations.

## What's said there

> "Yet the candid truth is that AI’s spatial capabilities remain far from human level. And the limits reveal themselves quickly." (section "Spatial Intelligence: The scaffolding of human cognition", text verified: yes)

Also verified, from the intro: "Yet they remain wordsmiths in the dark; eloquent but inexperienced, knowledgeable but ungrounded." (about LLMs)

- She grants that multimodal LLMs have "introduced some basics of spatial awareness": they analyze pictures and generate images and short videos.
- But she writes that state-of-the-art multimodal models "rarely perform better than chance on estimating distance, orientation, and size", can't navigate mazes or predict basic physics, and that AI-generated videos often lose coherence after a few seconds.
- Without spatial understanding, she argues, AI can't drive cars, guide robots in homes and hospitals, or speed up discovery in materials science and medicine.
- She closes the section with Wittgenstein ("the limits of my language mean the limits of my world"), adding "I’m not a philosopher. But I know at least for AI, there is more than just words."
- Earlier in the same section: everyday examples (parking a car, catching tossed keys, pouring coffee half asleep) and historical ones (Eratosthenes measuring the Earth with shadows, Watson and Crick building physical 3D models of DNA).
