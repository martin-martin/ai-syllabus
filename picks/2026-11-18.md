# AI 2027

**Who:** Daniel Kokotajlo, Scott Alexander, Thomas Larsen, Eli Lifland, Romeo Dean (AI Futures Project; Kokotajlo is an ex-OpenAI governance researcher)
**What / when:** forecast scenario (long-form essay), April 3, 2025, ai-2027.com
**Full piece:** https://ai-2027.com/
**Jump in here:** https://ai-2027.com/#late-2026-ai-takes-some-jobs (section "Late 2026: AI Takes Some Jobs", three short paragraphs plus a metrics box, ~2 min)

## Why this one

AI 2027 is a concrete, month-by-month scenario of how AI might develop, written in April 2025. It's now November 2026, so the section titled "Late 2026" describes, more or less, today. That makes it a rare chance to hold a widely-discussed forecast up against the present without waiting years. Current era after yesterday's foundational DQN paper, and a new set of people. The junior-developer and CS-degree line is a natural hook for an education audience.

## What's said there

> "The job market for junior software engineers is in turmoil: the AIs can do everything taught by a CS degree, but people who know how to manage and quality-control teams of AIs are making a killing." (section "Late 2026: AI Takes Some Jobs", text verified: yes)

> "AI has started to take jobs, but has also created new ones." (same section, text verified: yes)

> "Bigger than social media? Bigger than smartphones? Bigger than fire?" (same section, describing how people argue about how big AI is, text verified: yes)

- In the scenario, a fictional lab "OpenBrain" releases a cheap, easy-to-fine-tune model ("Agent-1-mini"), and the mainstream narrative shifts from "maybe the hype will blow over" to "guess this is the next big thing".
- Predictions for 2026 in that section: stock market up 30% for the year, junior SWE job market in turmoil, "familiarity with AI" as the top resume skill, a 10,000 person anti-AI protest in DC, the US DOD quietly scaling up contracts.
- A metrics box lists e.g. $1T global AI capex and 2.5% of US power going to AI in 2026.
- Context: the site added a note (Nov 22, 2025) saying "2027 was our modal (most likely) year at the time of publication", with their medians somewhat longer. So the authors themselves don't present the dates as firm.
