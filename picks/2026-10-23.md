# [1hr Talk] Intro to Large Language Models

**Who:** Andrej Karpathy, at OpenAI at the time (OpenAI founding member, later led AI at Tesla)
**What / when:** video talk (re-recorded at home), uploaded November 2023, YouTube
**Full piece:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjkBMFhNj_g
**Jump in here:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjkBMFhNj_g&t=683s (11:23–14:14, ~3 min)

## Why this one

Karpathy's intro talk is one of the most-watched general explanations of how LLMs work, and this short stretch is where he admits the limits of that understanding: we know the architecture in full detail, but not what the parameters are doing. His example is the "reversal curse" (the model knows Tom Cruise's mother, but not who her son is). It also ties back to the Anthropic interpretability post you just shared: here, in late 2023, he names mechanistic interpretability as the field trying to open the box, "to some extent but not fully right now". Different person and format from the last five picks (a video talk, after Hinton's Nobel lecture, an Anthropic blog post, 3Blue1Brown, Sutton's podcast).

## What's said there

> "this knowledge is weird and it's kind of one-dimensional" (13:03, transcript verified: yes)

> "they're not like a car where we sort of understand all the parts" (13:30, transcript verified: yes)

- 11:23: switches from "what does it produce" to "what goes on inside it". The Transformer architecture is fully understood, every mathematical operation known. The problem: "we don't actually really know what these 100 billion parameters are doing", we only know how to adjust them so next-word prediction gets better.
- 12:37: models seem to build "some kind of a knowledge database", but it's "very strange and imperfect". Example, the reversal curse: ask GPT-4 who Tom Cruise's mother is and it answers correctly (Mary Lee Pfeiffer); ask who her son is and it says it doesn't know. You have to ask "from a certain direction almost".
- 13:20: "think of LLMs as ... mostly inscrutable artifacts", products of a long optimization process. Mechanistic interpretability tries to figure out what the parts do, but for now we "treat them mostly as empirical artifacts": give inputs, measure outputs.
- 14:09: hence the need for "correspondingly sophisticated evaluations".
