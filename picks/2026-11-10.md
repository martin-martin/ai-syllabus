# Andrej Karpathy: AGI is still a decade away (Dwarkesh Podcast)

**Who:** Andrej Karpathy (OpenAI co-founder, former AI director at Tesla, now running Eureka Labs), interviewed by Dwarkesh Patel
**What / when:** podcast (video + full transcript), October 17, 2025, Dwarkesh Podcast (YouTube title: "We're summoning ghosts, not building animals")
**Full piece:** https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/andrej-karpathy (transcript) / https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY
**Jump in here:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXUZvyajciY&t=2447s (chapter "RL is terrible", 40:47–44:54, ~4 min; the LLM-judge "dhdhdhdh" story follows at 45:29–47:52)

## Why this one

Karpathy explains in plain words why reinforcement learning on LLMs is so wasteful, using a math student checking answers at the back of the book. It's a very teachable picture of credit assignment, and it pairs well with the Richard Sutton interview on the same podcast that Martin posted about on 2026-10-19: Sutton argues trial-and-error learning is the core of intelligence, Karpathy (a few weeks later) says "humans don't use reinforcement learning" and calls current RL "terrible", just better than what came before.

## What's said there

> "Reinforcement learning is terrible. It just so happens that everything that we had before it is much worse because previously we were just imitating people, so it has all these issues." (40:47, transcript verified: yes, official transcript on dwarkesh.com)

> "The way I like to put it is you're sucking supervision through a straw." (later in the same answer that starts at 40:47, transcript verified: yes)

> "Maybe the way I would put it is that humans don't use reinforcement learning, as I said." (40:47, opening line of the answer, transcript verified: yes)

- Example: a model gets a math problem and tries hundreds of attempts in parallel. You check "the back of the book", see that a few got the right answer, and RL upweights every single token of those attempts ("Do more of this"), including wrong alleys along the way. He calls that noise.
- "Sucking supervision through a straw": a minute of work, one number at the end, broadcast across the whole trajectory.
- A human would never do hundreds of rollouts, and would review which parts went well. "There's nothing in current LLMs that does this."
- He's still impressed by both steps that got us here: InstructGPT-style fine-tuning by imitation "blew my mind", and RL can find solutions a human might never come up with. "Yet, it's still stupid."
- Follow-up (45:29): process supervision with LLM judges is hard because judges are gameable. In one run the reward jumped to 100% and the outputs had turned into "dhdhdhdh", an adversarial example for the judge.

Andrej Karpathy (OpenAI co-founder, ex-AI director at Tesla) has a nice way of explaining what's off with how reinforcement learning trains LLMs.

Picture a student who tries a math problem hundreds of different ways and then checks the answer at the back of the book. A few attempts got it right. RL takes every step of those attempts, including the wrong turns along the way, and says "do more of this". A person would look back and figure out which parts actually helped.

It just so happens that everything that we had before it is much worse [...]"
