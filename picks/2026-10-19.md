# Richard Sutton – Father of RL thinks LLMs are a dead end

**Who:** Richard Sutton (reinforcement learning pioneer, 2024 Turing Award; University of Alberta / Amii), interviewed by Dwarkesh Patel
**What / when:** podcast (video + full transcript), 2025-09-26, Dwarkesh Podcast
**Full piece:** https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton (video: https://youtu.be/21EYKqUsPfg)
**Jump in here:** https://youtu.be/21EYKqUsPfg?t=2489 ("Surprises in the AI field", 41:29–46:41, ~5 min)

## Why this one

Sutton has been in AI since long before deep learning, and in this section he looks back at what surprised him: neural nets on language, and how the old "weak methods vs. strong methods" debate turned out. It's a compact bit of AI history from someone who was there, and it connects TD-Gammon to AlphaGo/AlphaZero to today. Nice contrast with the rest of the episode, where he argues LLMs are a dead end.

## What's said there

> "In the old days, it was interesting because things like search and learning were called weak methods because they're just using general principles, they're not using the power that comes from imbuing a system with human knowledge. Those were called strong. I think the weak methods have just totally won." (42:06, transcript verified: yes, from the transcript on dwarkesh.com)

- Biggest surprise for him: how effective neural networks are at language. "Language seemed different."
- In early AI, general-purpose methods (search, learning) were called "weak", knowledge-engineered symbolic systems "strong". He says learning and search "have just won the day", which he was always rooting for.
- AlphaGo was "merely a scaling up" of TD-Gammon (Gerry Tesauro's backgammon player using temporal difference learning), plus a new way of doing search. AlphaZero used TD learning; as a chess player he's impressed how it sacrifices material for positional advantage.
- On being out of sync with his field "perhaps decades": "I really view myself as a classicist rather than as a contrarian."
